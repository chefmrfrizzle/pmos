# PMOS public demonstration script

## Seven-minute narrative

1. **Boundary first.** Open **View data boundary**. The transaction and relationship paths are fictional; named capital institutions are real public identities; no private contact database is deployed.
2. **Asset readiness.** Introduce fictional opportunity PM-2026-014. Readiness is 82/100 because evidence beats completeness.
3. **Restitution exception.** Open the Evidence Room. Inspect the synthetic 1939–1946 custody gap, confidence, freshness, source, conflict, and specialist decision.
4. **Specialist judgment.** Demonstrate counsel-role escalation. PMOS structures the decision; it does not replace the specialist or counsel.
5. **Collector ranking.** Open Explain Ranking. Show factor weights and distinguish synthetic fit from a real relationship assertion.
6. **Institutional universe.** Filter VC, PE, hedge funds, sovereign wealth, CVC, pensions, and private banks. Open an official source. Private enrichment stays local.
7. **Close.** Open Deal Room and audit history. Export the synthetic brief. End with: “PMOS converts fragmented private-market evidence and institutional relationships into a transaction-ready operating layer while preserving specialist judgment.”

## Restitution scenario

The fictional artwork has a custody gap from 1939–1946. Synthetic checks support part of the chain, but evidence is insufficient for clearance. PMOS marks the claim `HUMAN REVIEW`, records the conflict, routes it to counsel, and preserves the decision in the audit history.

## David Rothschild meeting

Identity note: Sotheby’s lists David Rothschild as Head of Contemporary Art, Private Sales, UK and Global Lead, Private Sales Exhibitions. Do not confuse him with environmentalist David de Rothschild or use environmental personalization without direct confirmation.

### Opening

“PMOS is an evidence-backed operating layer for private markets. It turns fragmented object, relationship, transaction, and source signals into reviewable decisions—with every material claim tied to provenance, confidence, an owner, and a next action. It prepares specialist judgment; it does not replace it.”

### Discovery before demonstration

Ask four or five:

1. Where does a private-sale opportunity lose the most time: finding context, validating a claim, coordinating specialists, or getting a decision?
2. Before contacting a collector, what information must you trust, and where is it currently scattered?
3. Which handoff is most fragile: origination to specialist, specialist to client, or diligence to transaction?
4. Which error is costliest: a missed relationship, stale information, unsupported provenance, or duplicated outreach?
5. What must always remain human-approved?
6. What 30-day result would make you advocate internally?
7. Who owns workflow approval, security, data access, and procurement?

### Five-minute demonstration

1. Frame one decision: should this opportunity advance, who should engage, and what evidence supports it?
2. Show the relationship path and explain why each proposed connection exists.
3. Show the Deal Room: participants, missing evidence, risk, owner, and next action.
4. Open Evidence and Verification: first-party source, capture date, confidence, contradiction, reviewer decision, and audit history.
5. Close on the human-approved brief and the pilot scorecard.

### Pilot proposal

- 30 days
- one private-sale or exhibition workflow
- 3–5 users
- 25–50 redacted records or synthetic equivalents
- no more than two integrations
- weekly calibration
- no autonomous outreach or unapproved personal data

Agree success measures before starting: median brief-preparation time, first-party evidence coverage, reviewer agreement/overturn rate, stale or duplicate items caught, adoption, and zero unauthorized disclosure.

Commercial anchors are hypotheses until discovery confirms scope:

- $15,000 narrow design-partner pilot
- $25,000 standard 30-day pilot; credit it toward an annual agreement if agreed success criteria are met
- $25,000–$40,000 when integrations or security work expand
- $90,000–$150,000 annual starting range for one proven team/workflow

### Exact close

“David, based on what you said, the first problem worth proving is **[repeat his words]**. I propose a 30-day, 3–5-user pilot on **[one workflow]**, using redacted or synthetic data first. We’ll measure **[two measures he selected]**, and every material claim will remain human-reviewed and source-linked. The fixed fee is $25,000, credited toward an annual agreement if we meet the agreed criteria and continue. If that is directionally right, can we schedule a 45-minute scoping session with you, the workflow owner, and security/data this week—and leave that session with a signed pilot brief or a clear no-go?”

If price is the objection, ask whether the issue is budget, proof threshold, or scope. Narrow the scope to reach $15,000; do not cut price while leaving scope unchanged. If he is not the sponsor, ask for the named workflow owner, security/data owner, and an introduction with a decision date.
